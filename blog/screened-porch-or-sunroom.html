Screened Porch or Sunroom? How to Pick for a Grand Strand Home

By Tom Shipley, owner of Simply Enclosures · Myrtle Beach, SC · November 16, 2026

It's one of the first questions homeowners ask me at an estimate: "Should I screen it in, or close it in?" There's no one right answer. It comes down to how you want to use the space. Here's how I walk people through it.

The quick difference

A screened porch keeps the open air. You get the breeze and the sound of the outdoors, without the mosquitoes and no-see-ums. A sunroom closes the space in with windows or vinyl panels, so you can use it in more kinds of weather.

A screened porch is usually right if…

  • You mostly want to sit out from spring through fall.
  • You love the breeze and don't want to feel closed in.
  • The main problem is bugs, not cold or rain.
  • You want the simpler, quicker project.

A sunroom is usually right if…

  • You want to use the room in winter, too.
  • Rain blowing in is a real problem on your porch.
  • You want a spot for furniture, a TV, or plants year-round.
  • You're OK with a bigger project, and often a permit.

What I see on the Grand Strand

Living here, two things come up on almost every job:

  • Bugs. Mosquitoes and no-see-ums are the number one reason people call me. A good screen fixes that, whichever way you go.
  • Salt air and sun. Close to the water, everything gets a beating. That's why I use aluminum framing, which holds up in salt air, and why I always talk about upkeep before we start.

Also, you don't have to decide forever. A lot of screened porches can be upgraded to a sunroom later with windows or panels. When I come out, I'll look at your framing and tell you honestly what's possible.

4 questions to ask yourself before you get a quote

  1. When will I use it? Mostly warm months, or all year?
  2. What bothers me most right now? Bugs, rain, heat, or cold?
  3. What's going in there? Patio chairs, or furniture and electronics that need to stay dry?
  4. Does my HOA or town need to sign off? I'll tell you what your town requires, but it helps to check your HOA rules early.

If your answers are "warm months," "bugs" and "patio furniture," a screened porch is probably your best value. If you said "all year" or "rain," let's talk about a sunroom.
