How to Choose Your Porch Screen: Standard, No-See-Um or Pet Screen

By Tom Shipley, owner of Simply Enclosures · Myrtle Beach, SC · November 24, 2026

When we screen in a porch or re-screen an old one, one of the first choices is the screen itself. On the Grand Strand it usually comes down to three: standard screen, no-see-um screen and pet screen. Here's how I help people pick.

Standard screen

Our standard is charcoal fiberglass screen. It's easy to see through, and it keeps out mosquitoes and most flying bugs. For most porches, this is the right call.

Pick it if: you mainly want the mosquitoes out and you want the clearest view.

No-see-um screen

No-see-ums are the tiny biting bugs that come out at dusk, especially near the marsh. They can get through standard screen. No-see-um screen is a finer 20x20 weave that keeps them out.

Because the weave is tighter, it's slightly darker than standard screen, but you can still see out clearly. We bring samples to the estimate so you can see the difference yourself.

Pick it if: you live near the marsh or a creek, or you get bitten on the porch in the evening.

Pet screen

Dogs, cats and kids are hard on the bottom of a screen. Pet screen is a heavier, tougher screen that stands up to paws and claws much better than standard fiberglass.

A lot of people put pet screen (or a solid kick plate) only below the chair rail, where the damage happens, and keep standard screen up top for the view.

Pick it if: you have a dog that jumps at the screen, or a porch door that sees a lot of traffic.

Can I mix them?

Yes. A common setup is no-see-um or standard screen up top and pet screen or a kick plate below the chair rail. We'll go over it with you at the estimate.
