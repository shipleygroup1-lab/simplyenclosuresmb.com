Made for Carolina Forest neighborhoods

Carolina Forest is one of the fastest-growing parts of Horry County, with newer homes in planned neighborhoods off Carolina Forest Boulevard and Highway 501. A lot of these homes come with a covered back porch or lanai that is just begging to be screened in.

With the ponds, lakes and wooded lots here, mosquitoes can make an open porch hard to enjoy most of the year. Screening it in turns it into an extra room you will actually use.

Nearly every Carolina Forest neighborhood has an HOA. We give you a drawing with framing color and screen details for your application, and we can match the framing color to your home's trim so it looks like it was built with the house.

What Carolina Forest homeowners usually ask for

Screen in the builder's porch

Most homes already have a covered porch from the builder. Screening it in is the most common job we do here.

Kid- and pet-friendly

A chair rail with pet screen or a kick plate on the bottom keeps the lower screen safe from dogs and kids.

Patio enclosures

No covered porch? Ask about enclosing your patio or building a new screened space.

Options we build with

Standard or no-see-um screen

Our standard is charcoal fiberglass screen, which is easy to see through. If you get no-see-ums at dusk, ask about finer 20x20 no-see-um screen.

Pet screen and kick plates

Dogs and kids are hard on the bottom of a screen. A chair rail with tougher pet screen or a solid kick plate below it keeps the lower panels in one piece.

Framing color and doors

Powder-coated aluminum framing in colors that match your trim, with one or more screen doors placed where you actually walk.
