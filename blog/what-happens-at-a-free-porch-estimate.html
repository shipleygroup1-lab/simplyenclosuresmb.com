What Happens at a Free Screened Porch Estimate (and What to Have Ready)

By Tom Shipley, owner of Simply Enclosures · Myrtle Beach, SC · November 20, 2026

A lot of homeowners put off calling because they're not sure what an estimate involves, or they're worried about a sales pitch. Here's exactly what happens when I come out, so you know what to expect before I ever pull into your driveway.

Why I don't quote over the phone

People ask me for a ballpark all the time, and I understand why. But a screened porch is priced by the linear foot, and no two porches are the same. The number of openings, where the posts are, where you want the door, and how level the slab is all change the price. A phone guess is usually wrong one way or the other. I'd rather measure once and give you a real number.

What happens when I get there

  1. We walk the porch together. You tell me how you want to use it and what's bugging you now: mosquitoes, no-see-ums, pollen, rain.
  2. I measure every opening. Every one, on site. Nothing is guessed from a photo.
  3. We pick the details. Aluminum framing in white or bronze, regular fiberglass or pet-resistant screen, where the screen door goes, and a kick plate along the bottom.
  4. We talk about permits. Many screen-ins on an existing covered porch don't need one, but it depends on your town and HOA. I'll tell you what applies to your address.
  5. You get a clear written price. No pressure and no "today only" deal. Take your time.

5 things to have ready

  • Your HOA rules, if you have an HOA. Some want to approve the frame color.
  • A rough idea of the door. Which side do you walk out to the yard from?
  • Pets. If a dog or cat will be out there, pet-resistant screen is worth it.
  • Photos or ideas you like. A picture of a neighbor's porch helps a lot.
  • Everyone who's deciding. If two people are making the call, it's easiest if both can be there.

After the estimate

Once you sign, installs usually start within 5 days, unless it's a specialty product. When we finish, we walk through the porch with you to make sure everything opens, closes and looks right.
